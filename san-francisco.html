2026 - Installation - Receipt Printer, TouchDesigner, Raspberry Pi

How does surveillance capitalism affect community?

Process

The project started with a data export request sent to eleven platforms. The February 24th dataset returned 772 data points from a single ordinary day at home. To make the scale of that visible, a Python API in Blender was used to generate a 3D sculptural rendering of the dataset, and the findings were formatted as a transactional receipt, with platform names as vendor names, data events as line items, and payment method reading IDENTITY.

After feedback that the piece needed a more specific, eventful center, the focus shifted to a specific milestone: a first solo trip to San Francisco, March 13 through 21, 2026. The surveillance data from the trip was exported in the same process as the last iteration and mixed with journal entries written during the same week. A TouchDesigner network mapped the dataset to visual properties and composited the data visualization over trip footage, with journal text generating over the video in real time. A Tone.js file was created from the dataset to act as background music for the video.

To make the film feel made rather than generated, the TouchDesigner export was printed frame by frame, each frame physically cut by hand and scanned back in to build the stop motion animation. The misalignments and paper texture from the process are visible in the final film.

The final iteration introduced a second printer and a second archive into the film to intersect with my own archives. Historical displacement events in San Francisco, from the I-Hotel eviction in 1977 to the Waymo protests in 2024, were formatted in the same receipt structure as the personal surveillance data and overlaid with the testimonies of people who lived through them. The voices of protest appear in and out alongside the Tone.js audio in the video to further the intersection between archives. Two receipts now print simultaneously: one personal, one historical. The dialogue between them is the argument.

The installation setup went through its own round of iteration. The original plan was to place the two printers on pedestals of different heights, with the historical printer elevated on a taller pedestal behind a shorter one, so that the two receipts would descend along the same path and accumulate together on the floor. Material constraints changed the final configuration, but the intention of the two records converging in the same physical space remained central to the design.

To run both printers simultaneously, a Raspberry Pi was introduced to operate the historical printer autonomously, since a single laptop could only drive one printer at a time. The Pi runs the print script on boot, so the historical receipt starts automatically when the power strip is switched on, without any manual input.

Outcomes

The final spatial decisions came from thinking carefully about how the room itself could complete the installation. Cool spotlighting was introduced to draw attention to the receipts on the floor and create a sense of focus within the space. White curtains were laid on the floor and hung behind the screen to give the installation a defined boundary and make it feel like a complete environment rather than a collection of equipment. It also allowed for the hiding of all of the wiring needed for the installation. The two printers were placed on opposite sides of the space so that their receipts travel toward each other and intersect in the pile on the floor, the personal record and the historical record physically converging in the same accumulation.
